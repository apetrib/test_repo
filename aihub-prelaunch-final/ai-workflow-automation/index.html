Where the problem appears

Information repeatedly moves through the same manual steps.

Not every workflow can or should be automated. We look for processes that are repetitive, valuable and controllable enough for a pilot to demonstrate something useful.

Handoffs
Email → document → spreadsheet → follow-up.

Information is moved, copied and reformatted between people and systems.

Documents
Document intake & analysis.

Repeated reading, extraction, classification, routing or structuring.

Meetings
Meeting → actions → follow-up.

Recaps, actions, distribution and follow-up are rebuilt manually.

Recurring information
Summaries, reports and structured outputs.

Collection, retrieval, summarization and preparation of information that keeps recurring.

How we work

We understand the process before we build.

We start by understanding the current workflow, then select one pilot. We do not automate a process we do not yet understand.

Phase 1

Automation Discovery

We map the current workflow, people, handoffs, inputs, outputs and systems involved.

  • effort, errors and rework;
  • privacy / security;
  • technical feasibility;
  • estimated business value;
  • prioritization: value × frequency × feasibility × risk.
Phase 2

One-Workflow Pilot

We build a limited pilot with clear boundaries and criteria before implementation.

  • clear inputs and outputs;
  • defined users;
  • defined systems / tools;
  • agreed success criteria;
  • recommendation: scale / modify / stop.
Relevant experience

Relevant experience includes automating analytics infrastructure by migrating processes into Dataiku, installing the platform and enabling the team, as well as developing analytical workflows for recommendation, segmentation and prediction. The principle is the same: process and success criteria first, technology second.

Pilot logic

Input → AI / Logic → Human Review → Output

01Input

Data, documents or the workflow trigger.

02AI / Logic

Processing, rules, transformation or orchestration.

03Human Review

Human control where risk or context requires it.

04Output

A clear, usable and measurable output.

Measurement

We establish a baseline before discussing results.

We do not invent savings percentages. Where it makes sense, we compare the pilot with the current state.
